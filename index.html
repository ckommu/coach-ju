About Me

About Me

Photo

My fitness journey started in high school, where it was just massive amounts of cardio on a calorie deficit. I was constantly hungry and weak.

During undergrad, I did a lot of reading online about strength training and how much better it works than cardio alone. I had no idea I'd keep going with it. Not just for losing weight, but for everything else it turned out to do.

Grad school is when fitness became a much more pertinent part of my life. I was extremely busy juggling school and a part-time job. I was getting no sleep and everything I was already managing mental health-wise was spiraling. The only thing that kept me together was my weekly fitness routine.

Resistance training made me a better learner. It showed me that when I face challenges, they're temporary setbacks and not intrinsic failures. It built a confidence I'd never had. It changed my whole identity where fitness stopped being a short-term goal before the summer and became a lifestyle.

I didn't always stick to it in the beginning. I relied on motivation and willpower, and both inevitably ran out. It took years to learn how to build a system that works. Then I stopped restarting and just kept progressing.

During those years I wished I had someone to help me navigate it. Someone who could fight the shame and perfectionism in my head. Someone who would meet me where I was, not when I felt “ready.” Because that “right” season never comes.

That's what I teach now. Training, mindset, and a plan that survives a bad week.
